# Chapter 1: Introduction and Motivation — summary

[BOOK: mathematics-for-machine-learning, ch.1] The chapter answers "what is machine learning, in words precise enough to build on?" Its answer: systems built from **data** (represented as vectors), a **model** (a parametrized function or probability distribution), and **learning** (choosing parameters by optimizing a performance measure on training data, judged on unseen data). §1.1 fixes this vocabulary and warns that words like "algorithm" carry two meanings (predictor vs training procedure). §1.2 gives the two reading strategies — bottom-up (foundations first: reliable, but motivation-starved) and top-down (problem first: motivated, but resting on undefined terms) — and explains the book's two-part modular structure that supports both: Part I (Ch. 2–7) lays foundations (linear algebra, analytic geometry, matrix decompositions, vector calculus, probability, optimization), Part II applies them to four pillars (regression Ch. 9, dimensionality reduction Ch. 10, density estimation Ch. 11, classification Ch. 12), with Chapter 8 restating data/models/learning mathematically in between. §1.3 notes exercises and feedback channels (mml-book.com). This hub mirrors the structure: bottom-up course-map ordering, top-down "why it exists" opening in every topic.
